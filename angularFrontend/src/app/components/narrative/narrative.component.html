This is how you actually learn to speak Spanish

Most of us learned Spanish the same way.

Workbooks. Conjugation drills. Vocab lists. Maybe Duolingo on the commute. And yet — when a native speaker starts talking, the words disappear.

That's not a you problem. That's a method problem.

There's a fundamental difference between learning about Spanish and learning to speak Spanish. One happens in a classroom. The other happens in conversation — with real people, in real time, over and over again until it clicks.

I know this firsthand. I took Spanish in elementary school, high school, and college. But I didn't truly learn to speak it until I did an immersion program in Oaxaca, Mexico. When I came back, I noticed something that was mind-boggling to me: friends who had taken far more Spanish classes than I had were still freezing up, still second-guessing themselves, still afraid to open their mouths.

The difference wasn't grammar. It was confidence — and confidence only comes from doing.

Speaking with native speakers regularly takes the fear out of the mistakes. It makes you comfortable with being imperfect. It rewires your brain from performing Spanish to living it.

I've spent years building relationships with brilliant native speakers across Latin America — and I've brought the best of them in to teach our learners. Watching those connections form, watching someone go from nervous and stuck to speaking freely — that's the full circle moment. And it can be yours too.
